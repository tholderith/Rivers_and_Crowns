Rivers & Crowns

Lead a nation's expeditions through a contested river basin, in a fictional region inspired by northeastern North America around 1700. Move heroes, grow settlements, recruit troops, sign agreements and decide which crossings and trade routes to hold.

Explore → find an opportunity → fight or negotiate → gain resources → upgrade the settlement → recruit something exciting.

Can I paint art like this? No.

I can't generate painted images. My pixel scenes in the first draft were placeholders, and they will never reach the level of your concepts. Use ChatGPT, or another image model, for the art. That is the right tool for it.

What I can do with it

  • Build the real game UI, like the two working mock-ups below, using your art.
  • Turn generated sheets into game sprites. I cut the units, landmarks and resources below out of your asset sheet and removed the backgrounds.
  • Write the design, the prompts, and the game code.

One rule for the art

Ask the image model for art with no UI and no text in it: scenes, buildings and units on plain backgrounds. Menus, numbers and buttons are built in code so they stay sharp and can change. The prompts further down follow this rule.

Rivers are the signature

  • Boats move armies fast downstream and slowly upstream.
  • Fords and bridges are chokepoints, and portages link separate river systems.
  • A small post at the right junction matters more than a big fort far away.
  • Independent communities can grant passage, trade or recruits through agreements.

Heroes and Command

  • Specialties: commander, navigator, diplomat, engineer, quartermaster.
  • Command points replace mana: Rally, Coordinated Volley, Forced March, Fieldworks, Feigned Retreat.
  • Equipment includes instruments, fine weapons, dispatches and charts. A chart can reveal a shortcut.

Combat

  • Small hex fields, readable stacks, and 5–10 minute battles.
  • Fire, reload, maneuver: firing opens a window for the enemy to advance or flank.
  • Cover, elevation, morale and short sight lines.
  • Each unit has a job: hold, scout, skirmish, breach or support. Higher tiers are not just better musketeers.

Settlements

  • 8–12 buildings per faction. Every one visibly changes the town painting.
  • A few upgrades branch, for example Wharf → Commercial Harbor or Military Dock.
  • Each building needs a "before" and "after" art layer. Plan for this in the art budget.

Four resources

  • Coin, timber, provisions and iron.
  • Trade goods give bonuses but are not a fifth resource to manage.

Deferred

  • Naval combat, detailed seasons, online multiplayer and long campaigns.
Prototype

One river, 30–45 minutes

Two playable factions and one independent community. Three heroes per side, five unit types and eight buildings per faction. One river map with a ford, a portage, a trading settlement and a fort. You win by holding the key routes or taking the enemy headquarters. Local hotseat play and a basic AI.

The art runs in parallel: lock a style guide, then generate one sprite sheet per milestone. Unit animation (walking, firing) is the hardest part for image models. Budget for a pixel artist or keep animation minimal.
